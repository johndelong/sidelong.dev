The league was my mom's idea. She wanted a way to get the grandkids into fantasy football without putting them in front of a screen or teaching them every rule of the game. Just pick some teams and follow them for a whole season. Five kids play, and Grandma and Grandpa play too. We drew names from a hat for the draft order, then took turns over FaceTime picking four NFL teams each.

The scoring happens on paper, on purpose. My wife asked me why, too. If I built an app that just gave them the score, the kids wouldn't have any reason to learn anything. Writing down the score of every game and working out the points they earned from it is what gets them noticing the little things: which teams are good, which ones are struggling, and how the whole thing works. Just handing them the information would take away the chance to learn and get invested.

The software started as a way to make those sheets. Every player needs their own four teams, every opponent for all 18 weeks, and the byes in the right places, which is a data problem more than a design one. Writing a small program was far easier than laying it all out by hand in Canva. Most of it I built by typing to [Rhys](/apps/rhys), our household assistant, from the road.

It became a live site once I realized I didn't always trust my kids' math. It also helps when someone forgets to fill in a few weeks, since everything they need is in one place, and it keeps track of how everyone compares when it's time to hand out mid-season prizes.

The plan was for the cousins to compare sheets over FaceTime every week. That hasn't really stuck. Instead, every couple of weeks I help my girls fill in their scores, and then I tell them where their cousins stand.

Grandma and Grandpa bought trophies for first and last place. Each one goes home with a kid for a year, then gets handed on next season. The last-place trophy is a silly statue that says "you stink." We've been careful to explain that they don't stink. Their picks did.

The rules may change from year to year, and I want the app to grow with the kids. Right now it's built entirely around our family, but I might open it up so other families can run a league of their own. I also built a season outlook that simulates the rest of the schedule and gives everyone a chance of winning, mostly to see if I could. I'm still deciding whether it belongs in front of the kids. When your chance is close to zero, it isn't much fun to look at.
