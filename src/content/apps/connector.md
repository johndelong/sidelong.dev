Connector started as a napkin idea on a work trip to Jacksonville, Florida. Pete, Ryan and I worked together at Michigan Software Labs, and we were trading app ideas when we landed on one that sounded like a blast: a cocktail recipe app that felt fancy, animated and immersive, not just another list of ingredients.

The name comes from a room at Michigan Software Labs. The Connector sat between the two partners' offices and joined them together. It's where we met to celebrate, share life and spend time together, usually with a cocktail in hand, since it had a fully stocked liquor cabinet. A drinks app named after the room that brought us together felt right.

Pete was the idea guy. Ryan designed the app and made a lot of its assets. I ended up as the glue between the three of us: I wrote most of the code and did some of the design in Figma, including the glassware.

The part we cared about most was seeing the drink. You can still read a recipe as a list, but Connector also pours each ingredient into the glass as its own layer, so you can see the ratios at a glance instead of working them out from measurements. Drawing everything ourselves, rather than using photos, also meant the liquid could move, with a gentle wave inside the glass.

It's on the App Store, and a few people use it. Honestly, since AI came along, I've mostly stopped using it myself. When I want a drink, I ask an AI, and then I can never find that recipe again when I want it. That's the next chapter: a place to save the good ones, and help building a drink from whatever's in the pantry. With the tools available now, I'd also like to redesign parts of the app and make the glassware and animations far more realistic and immersive. It could use some love.
