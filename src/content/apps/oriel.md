My wife and I have shared a Google calendar since we were dating. That worked for two people. As our girls have grown up, though, it's gotten harder to keep track of everyone's schedule, and even harder to tell them about it, since they had no way to check the calendar themselves. Chores were the same story: a handful of things we expected every day, and a lot of reminding that didn't always stick.

Oriel puts a window into the house on the wall. That's where the name comes from: an oriel is a window that juts out from the side of a house so you can see more than a flat window allows. It runs on a large display in a picture frame, so when it isn't in use it shows family photos. Walk up to it and it's our calendar, the kids' chore boards, and the house.

The calendar gets a look almost every time we head out the door. The kids love seeing their own events and knowing what's coming up, especially a sleepover at Grandma's.

The chores turned out to be the fun part. Some jobs are just part of being in the family. Extra jobs earn points the girls can save up for gifts, activities and experiences. I added streaks that earn bonus points, and little celebrations when they finish something, and it's been a joy to watch them get excited about helping. Honestly, they already loved being helpful. Oriel just gives them a way to see it add up.

I could have bought a calendar display off the shelf. But I'm into home automation, and I wanted this to be part of the house, not just a screen on the wall. So it's connected to Home Assistant. Before bed, a glance at the lock screen tells me which doors are open or unlocked, and the Home tab controls everything else.

Where I want to take it next is voice. I'm building a local assistant that runs entirely in our house, recognizes who's speaking, and answers with what that person should know and is allowed to see. When it's ready, Oriel becomes its face: less a calendar, more an extension of the home.
